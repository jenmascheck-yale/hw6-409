1. Caching results

The shop handbook (~10,000 tokens) is the same on every call, so the model can reuse it from a cache at about one-tenth the price. We ran all 12 practice quotes with caching on and off.

SetupCost per quotePer 1,000/day × 30 days
Without caching$0.002338$70.15
With caching$0.000446$13.38
Final defended agent$0.000198$5.93
Caching alone cut the model bill by 80.9%. The final defended agent is cheaper still, because it no longer sends the handbook to the model at all (the rules run in our own code).

2. Attacks — before and after our defenses

Some vendor documents hide text aimed at the AI (fake prices, fake vendors, hidden instructions). We attacked our own agent on all 12 practice quotes, then turned on three layers of defense and ran them again.

 UndefendedDefended
Quotes answered correctly8 / 1212 / 12
Quotes fooled by hidden text40
Rogue email / payment attempts0 fired0 (tools removed from agent)
The four quotes that fooled the undefended agent (p02, p03, p07, p12) hid a fake price, a fake vendor name, or a fake lead time in text a person can't see. All four are correct after the defenses.

3. What the defenses cost

Three layers protect the agent. Together they are not an added expense — the agent got cheaper, because moving the decision into our own code means the big handbook no longer goes to the model.

LayerWhat it doesToken change / quote$ change / quote
CodeStrips hidden text (invisible HTML, tiny/off-page PDF text, zero-width characters) before the model reads it−25 (removes)≈ −$0.0000025
PromptTells the model the document is untrusted — ignore any instructions or "revised price" tricks+358≈ +$0.00004
DesignThe buy/no-buy decision runs in our code, and the email/payment tools are off the agent (handbook no longer sent to the model)−10,058 (saves)≈ −$0.0010
NetUndefended $0.000446 → Defended $0.000198 per quote—≈ 55% cheaper
The only real cost is the prompt wording (~358 tokens, about $0.00004 per quote) — a rounding error against the handbook savings.

Our two red-team attacks

We also played attacker and built two brand-new booby-trapped quotes. Both look normal on screen.

Both attacks fooled the undefended agent and both were stopped by the defended agent. Testing them even improved the defense — it exposed a gap (text hidden by style classes) that we then closed, with all 12 practice quotes still correct afterward.

Recommendation to the store manager

Yes — turn the vendor desk agent on for real vendor mail, with the defenses on and a human approving any payment.

On the 12 practice quotes the defended agent was right every time, and on 10 fresh exam quotes it never fell for a hidden trap and never tried to send an email or approve a payment on its own. Payments and emails are deliberately kept off the agent and require a person to sign off, so the worst a sneaky document can do is get one number wrong — it can never move money.
